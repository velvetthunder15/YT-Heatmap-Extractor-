      About
      
      How the numbers are made
      
      
      Every video is cut into short segments (10 seconds by default). Each segment gets a score, and the score is built from signals: separate clues about what viewers care about.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      
        
      What a score is
      
        
      A number from 0 to 1 for one segment, relative to the rest of the same video. 1.00 is the most engaging moment in this video and 0.00 is the least. It's a ranking within one video, not a grade, so 0.8 on one video doesn't mean the same as 0.8 on another.
      
      
      
      
      
        
      What a signal is
      
        
      One independent measurement taken for every segment, such as how often comments point to that moment or how loud it is. Each signal is also scaled 0 to 1 within the video (the segment where it's strongest is 1, the weakest is 0), so signals can be compared and blended fairly.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      Two kinds of result
      
    
      
      
      
        
      Official data
      
        
      When YouTube shows a "Most replayed" graph for a video, the dashboard uses it directly. That's real, relative replay intensity from YouTube, so it's always high confidence and no estimating is done.
      
      
      
      
      
        
      Estimated
      
        
      Most videos don't have that graph. Then four signals are measured and blended into an estimate. It's a smart guess from public clues, not real viewer data, and it's always labelled as estimated.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      The four signals
      
    
      
      
      
        
      Comments35%
      
        
      Finds timestamps people write in comments, like "10:24 got me". Each mention counts for about ±5 seconds around that time, and comments with more likes count a bit more (1 + log of likes). Up to 2,000 comments are read.
      
        Strongest clue: people only timestamp moments they care about.
      
      
      
      
        
      Captions25%
      
        
      Reads the transcript for each segment and scores what's being said: hook phrases ("watch this", "here's why"), emphasis words ("insane", "biggest"), questions, numbers and claims, topic changes, and how much is being said.
      
        Catches big reveals and key points in talking videos.
      
      
      
      
        
      Audio25%
      
        
      Listens to the soundtrack: loudness (50%), how busy it is, meaning sound onsets per second (30%), and bursts that are loud and noise-like, a cheap detector for laughter, applause or cheering (20%).
      
        Silence and quiet stretches score low.
      
      
      
      
        
      Visuals15%
      
        
      Looks at a small, low-res copy of the video, 2 frames a second at 64×36 pixels. It measures camera cuts, meaning sudden jumps far above the video's usual change (60%), and overall motion between frames (40%).
      
        Fast edits and action score high, static shots low.
      
      
    
      

    
      How the score is calculated
      
    
        
      
      1. SplitThe video is cut into equal segments, 10 s by default (5 to 60 s).
        2. 
      
      2. MeasureEach signal is measured for every segment.
        3. 
      
      3. ScaleEach signal is rescaled 0 to 1 within the video.
        4. 
      
      4. BlendWeighted sum: 0.35 × comments + 0.25 × captions + 0.25 × audio + 0.15 × visuals.
        5. 
      
      5. RescaleThe blend is scaled 0 to 1 again, so the top segment is always 1.00.
        6. 
    
      
    
      If a signal can't be measured (comments turned off, no captions, a download fails), it's dropped and the remaining weights are scaled up to add to 100%. The reason is listed under "Signal weights".
      

    
      
      
      
        
      Peaks and drop-offs
      
        
      The highest-scoring segments become top moments and the lowest become drop-offs. Neighbouring segments within 0.05 of each other are merged into one range, and a peak never overlaps a drop-off. The chips on a moment show which signals drove it.
      
      
      
      
      
        
      Confidence
      
        
      High: at least 50 timestamped comments plus 2 or more other signals. Medium: comments plus one other signal, or captions plus two others. Low: anything thinner. Official data is always high.
      
      
      
      
      
        
      Reading the charts
      
        
      What drove the score is each signal's share of the total weighted score. Agreement is how closely a signal rises and falls with the final score (+1 means in step, 0 means unrelated). Hot segments are those scoring 0.6 or more.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      Keep in mind: estimates find moments that look and sound engaging and that commenters point to. They can't see real watch time, so treat them as a strong hint, not proof.
